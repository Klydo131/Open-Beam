# Start here

Open Beam lets you bring your own spreadsheets and data files together, look
at them, chart them, share them with colleagues, and take them into Excel,
Google Sheets or Power BI. You do not need to be technical to use it.

## Get Open Beam running

1. Open the [Releases page](https://github.com/Klydo131/Open-Beam/releases)
   and download the file for your computer:
   - Windows: `…-windows-x64.zip`
   - Mac with Apple silicon: `…-macos-apple-silicon.tar.gz`
   - Linux: `…-linux-x64.tar.gz`
2. Unzip it and double-click **beam** (on Windows, **beam.exe**).
3. Your browser opens Open Beam. Keep the small window that appeared open
   while you work; close it to stop Open Beam.

> **Until the first release is published** the Releases page is empty, and
> Open Beam has to be built from source. See [Install Open Beam](01-install.md).
>
> The downloads are not yet signed by a publisher, so Windows may say
> "Windows protected your PC" (choose **More info → Run anyway**) and macOS may
> refuse to open it (right-click **beam**, choose **Open**, then **Open** again).

Open Beam keeps everything it needs in a folder called **OpenBeam** in your
home folder. Your data never leaves your computer.

## The first time

1. Choose a username and password. You are the administrator.
2. Select **Add data** and pick a CSV, Excel (.xlsx) or Parquet file.
3. That's it: your table appears. From there:
   - **Chart** turns it into bars or a line.
   - **Download Excel** or **Download CSV** takes exactly what you see.
   - **Sharing** lets colleagues with an account see it too.

## Basic or Advanced

Everything above works in **Basic**, the default. If you want more control,
open **Settings** and choose **Advanced**: you can pick which columns to show,
use more filters, name your datasets, and copy ready-made connections for
Excel and Power BI. You can switch back at any time; it only changes what you
see, never what you are allowed to do.
